# Theme codebook for consultation response tagging

This codebook is a practical starter for tagging consultation comments into themes. In a real consultation, this would usually be developed at the start of the process with subject matter experts (SMEs), policy leads, and stakeholders so that the labels reflect the council's strategic priorities and local context.

The goal is not to create a perfect taxonomy from day one. The aim is to create a clear, consistent, manageable set of theme labels that can be refined as the coding progresses.

## Core principles

- Tag the main idea of the comment, not every minor detail.
- Use broad themes first unless there is a clear need for a more specific label.
- Use up to 2 themes per comment when a response contains more than one idea.
- If the comment is too vague to assign confidently, use `Other / unclear`.
- Avoid over-splitting; it is easier to refine categories later than to merge a messy scheme.

## Theme list

### 1. Children and young people
Use for issues relating to children, teenagers, families, schools, childcare, youth services, safety, wellbeing, and early years.

Examples:
- school funding
- youth clubs
- childcare and nursery support
- support for families
- mental health services for young people
- meals and nutrition for children
- activities and play spaces for children
- academy schools

### 2. Education and skills
Use for comments about schools, learning, skills, training, employability, apprenticeships, universities, and education access.

Examples:
- school improvements
- teacher recruitment
- adult education
- vocational training
- skills and apprenticeships

### 3. Health and wellbeing
Use for general health, mental health, physical wellbeing, carers, patient care, and access to health services.

Examples:
- mental health support
- GP access
- healthy food
- public health
- wellbeing services

### 4. Housing and homelessness
Use for affordable housing, homelessness, housing quality, access to homes, private rent, and housing support.

Examples:
- affordable homes
- homelessness prevention
- social housing
- repairs and maintenance
- temporary accommodation

### 5. Transport and travel
Use for roads, public transport, walking, cycling, parking, connectivity, accessibility, and travel inequality.

Examples:
- bus services
- cycle routes
- road safety
- disabled access
- congestion

### 6. Environment and climate
Use for pollution, waste, climate resilience, flood risk, green space, biodiversity, and sustainability.

Examples:
- recycling
- flood prevention
- clean air
- tree planting
- zero waste strategy

### 7. Jobs and local economy
Use for employment, wages, local business support, town centre improvement, skills investment, and economic development.

Examples:
- local jobs
- economic development
- support for local businesses
- growth and investment
- apprenticeship opportunities

### 8. Community and local services
Use for local infrastructure, public services, civic support, community facilities, public spaces, libraries, and services for residents.

Examples:
- libraries
- community centres
- parks
- civic services
- local amenities

### 9. Public services and council delivery
Use for comments about council processes, service quality, transparency, accountability, communication, and organisational performance.

Examples:
- council coordination
- joined-up services
- funding decisions
- accountability
- service efficiency

### 10. Equality, inclusion, and access
Use for disability, inclusion, fairness, accessibility, discrimination, older adults, and barriers to services.

Examples:
- accessible services
- equal opportunities
- support for disabled residents
- inclusion
- tackling inequality

### 11. Community safety and crime
Use for anti-social behaviour, policing, crime prevention, neighbourhood safety, and public protection.

Examples:
- crime prevention
- safety in public spaces
- anti-social behaviour
- neighbourhood security

### 12. Other / unclear
Use when the comment is too vague, off-topic, or does not provide enough detail to assign a meaningful theme.

Examples:
- not sure
- no idea
- vague comment
- not relevant

## Example tagging

### Example 1
Comment: "We need more youth clubs with mental health support workers available for young people during school holidays."

Tags:
- Children and young people
- Health and wellbeing

### Example 2
Comment: "Improve local bus routes and make them more reliable for older residents and young people."

Tags:
- Transport and travel
- Equality, inclusion, and access

### Example 3
Comment: "The council should publish a zero-waste strategy with recycling and repair options."

Tags:
- Environment and climate
- Public services and council delivery

### Example 4
Comment: "Not sure."

Tags:
- Other / unclear

## Notes for real-world development

In a real consultation, this codebook would usually be developed early in the project with SMEs and policy stakeholders. The process would typically involve:

- reviewing the consultation questions,
- identifying the main strategic outcome areas,
- agreeing a consistent label set,
- testing the codebook on a small pilot sample,
- refining categories and definitions before full coding.

This helps ensure that the final themes reflect both policy priorities and the actual language used by residents.

## Recommended next step

Start with the broad categories above and only split them further when the data clearly requires it. A simple, stable codebook is usually better than an over-complex one.
